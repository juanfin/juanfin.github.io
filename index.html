1. Comparable Company Analysis

Finance / US technology sector / fiscal year 2025

Objective

The objective of this analysis is to verify whether the profitability obtained by Amazon in fiscal year 2025 is in line with the profitability obtained by comparable companies in the US technology sector during the same period.

Method

Amazon was selected as the tested party. Seven listed US technology companies were selected as comparables, and four profit level indicators were calculated for each company from its audited financial statements.

Since it is not possible to adjust for all the differences between the tested party and the comparable companies, the results are based on the interquartile range, which runs from the lower quartile to the upper quartile of the comparables' results, and not on the full range.

Tested party
Amazon
Comparables
Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, Meta Platforms, Apple, Broadcom, ServiceNow
Indicators
Gross margin, operating margin, return on assets (ROA), return on equity (ROE)
Source
U.S. SEC EDGAR, Form 10-K filings

Results

The interquartile range of each indicator for the comparable companies is presented below, together with the result obtained by Amazon.

Table 1. Gross margin

Tested partyResultInterquartile range: comparable companies
Gross marginLower quartileMedianUpper quartile
Amazon50.3%63.7%68.8%74.3%

Calculations: own, from Form 10-K filings.

Amazon's gross margin is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies. This implies that a larger part of each dollar of revenue is absorbed by the direct cost of what the company sells: Amazon's cost of sales includes the purchase price of the products it resells and their shipping, whereas most of the comparable companies sell software, advertising or chip designs, whose unit cost is low. For Amazon it means less room to cover its operating expenses before reaching a profit.

Table 2. Operating margin

Tested partyResultInterquartile range: comparable companies
Operating marginLower quartileMedianUpper quartile
Amazon11.2%32.0%39.9%43.5%

Calculations: own, from Form 10-K filings.

Amazon's operating margin is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies. Amazon keeps about 11 cents of operating income for each dollar of revenue, against 40 cents for the median comparable. The distance to the median widens from 18.5 percentage points at the gross margin level to 28.7 points at the operating level, which implies that operating expenses, and not only the cost of sales, weigh more on Amazon than on the comparable companies. It also means that its operating income is more sensitive to changes in costs or in sales volume.

Table 3. Return on assets (ROA)

Tested partyResultInterquartile range: comparable companies
ROALower quartileMedianUpper quartile
Amazon9.5%15.0%16.5%26.7%

Calculations: own, from Form 10-K filings.

Amazon's return on assets is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies. ROA is the product of the net margin and the asset turnover. Amazon generates 0.88 dollars of revenue per dollar of assets, above the comparables' median of 0.55, so it uses its assets intensively. The lower ROA comes from its net margin: 10.8% against a median of 32.8%. This implies that each dollar invested in the business returns less profit than in the comparable companies, even though the asset base is used more.

Table 4. Return on equity (ROE)

Tested partyResultInterquartile range: comparable companies
ROELower quartileMedianUpper quartile
Amazon18.9%28.1%29.6%54.1%

Calculations: own, from Form 10-K filings.

Amazon's return on equity is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies. Its financial leverage is similar to that of the comparables (assets of 1.99 times equity, against a median of 1.80), so the difference again comes from the margin and not from the capital structure. Amazon also retains its earnings instead of paying dividends, which keeps its equity base growing, while some comparables reduce theirs through share buybacks (Apple's ROE of 151.9% is the clearest case). This implies a lower return on the book value of the shareholders' investment.

Conclusion

Based on the results obtained, it was possible to verify that the four profit level indicators of Amazon for fiscal year 2025 are below the interquartile range of the comparable companies.

From this it can be inferred that, during fiscal year 2025, Amazon obtained a lower profitability than the selected comparable companies, both on its sales (gross and operating margin) and on the resources it employs (ROA and ROE).

This result should be read together with the differences in business mix that could not be adjusted: a large part of Amazon's revenue comes from retail, an activity with lower margins than the software and semiconductor activities of most of the comparable companies.

2. EBITDA Multiples

Finance / US technology sector / prices at 31 December 2025

Objective

The objective of this analysis is to estimate a reference value per share for Amazon by applying the EBITDA multiple valuation method, and to verify whether the multiple at which Amazon trades is in line with the multiples of comparable companies in the US technology sector.

Method

The enterprise value to EBITDA multiple (EV / EBITDA) was calculated for Amazon and for seven comparable companies, using closing share prices at 31 December 2025 and the financial statements for fiscal year 2025. The interquartile range of the comparables' multiples was then applied to Amazon's EBITDA to obtain an implied enterprise value, equity value and value per share.

Company valued
Amazon
Comparables
Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, Meta Platforms, Apple, Broadcom, ServiceNow
Multiples
EV / EBITDA, with EV / Revenue as a cross-check
Sources
U.S. SEC EDGAR, Form 10-K filings; Yahoo Finance closing prices

Amazon's EV / EBITDA multiple is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies.

Conclusion

Based on the application of the EBITDA multiple valuation method, it was possible to verify that Amazon's multiple of 16.8x is below the interquartile range of the comparable companies (23.9x to 42.5x). Applying that range to Amazon's EBITDA gives a value per share between USD 326.06 and USD 576.79, with USD 388.41 at the median, against a market price of USD 230.82.

A multiple below the range of the comparable companies can be read in two ways:

  • The first, that the share is undervalued against its sector, in which case the median of the comparables would be the reference for its value.
  • The second, that the lower multiple reflects lower expected margins and growth than those of the comparable companies, in which case the company's own multiple is the appropriate reference.

Given that Amazon's EBITDA margin (20.3%) is well below the median of the comparables (50.7%), and that most of them are software and semiconductor companies, the second reading carries more weight here. The implied value illustrates the method; it is not a price target or investment advice.

3. DCF

Finance / discounted cash flow / valuation date 31 December 2025

Objective

The objective of this analysis is to estimate the value of Amazon and a reference value per share by applying the discounted cash flow (DCF) method, and to compare that value with the market price at 31 December 2025.

Method

The unlevered free cash flows of the company were projected for five years (2026 to 2030) and discounted at the weighted average cost of capital (WACC). A terminal value was added for the years after 2030, calculated with the Gordon growth model. Debt was then deducted and cash added to the resulting enterprise value to obtain the equity value and the value per share.

Company valued
Amazon
Projection
2026 to 2030, three scenarios: conservative, base and optimistic
Discount rate
WACC, with the cost of equity from the CAPM
Sources
U.S. SEC EDGAR, Form 10-K filings; FRED; Yahoo Finance

Conclusion

Based on the application of the discounted cash flow method, the value per share of Amazon under the base scenario is USD 92.87, with a range of USD 70.75 to USD 117.71 between the conservative and optimistic scenarios, against a market price of USD 230.82 at 31 December 2025.

The value obtained is below the market price in the three scenarios and across the whole sensitivity table. From this it can be inferred that the market price reflects expectations that are more favourable than the premises of this model: higher operating margins, a faster reduction of capital expenditures after the current investment cycle, or a lower discount rate.

Two features of the result should be kept in mind. Capital expenditures reached 18.4% of sales in 2025, which leaves little free cash flow in the first projected years, and the terminal value represents 80.5% of the enterprise value, so the result depends heavily on the premises for the years after 2030. The value illustrates the method; it is not a price target or investment advice.

4. LBO

Finance / leveraged buyout / hypothetical transaction at 31 December 2025

Objective

The objective of this analysis is to estimate the return that a financial sponsor would obtain from acquiring Dropbox through a leveraged buyout (LBO), holding it for five years and selling it at the end of 2030. The transaction is hypothetical and was built to illustrate the method.

Method

In a leveraged buyout, a sponsor acquires a company using a large proportion of debt and a smaller proportion of its own equity. During the holding period the cash flow generated by the company is used to repay that debt, so the sponsor's return comes from three sources: the growth of EBITDA, the repayment of debt, and the multiple at which the company is sold.

Dropbox was selected because it has the profile usually sought for this type of transaction: stable subscription revenue, high margins and low capital expenditure requirements. The purchase price was set at a premium over the market price, the existing debt was refinanced, and the free cash flow of each year was applied to repay the term loan.

Target
Dropbox
Holding period
Five years, 2026 to 2030
Return measures
Internal rate of return (IRR) and multiple of invested capital (MOIC)

Conclusion

Based on the premises of the model, the acquisition of Dropbox at USD 36.14 per share would give the sponsor an internal rate of return of 12.1% and a multiple of 1.77 times the equity invested, with an exit at the end of 2030 at the same multiple paid at entry.

The return comes from two sources. EBITDA grows from USD 846.6 million to USD 1,123.7 million, which at a constant multiple raises the enterprise value by about USD 3,810 million, and the company repays USD 1,813.5 million of the term loan with its own cash flow, which lowers the net debt from 6.0 to 2.7 times EBITDA. No part of the return comes from selling at a higher multiple.

From this it can be inferred that the transaction would not be attractive at this price for a sponsor that requires a return of around 20%: the entry multiple of 13.7x and the 30% premium leave the IRR only modestly above the cost of the debt used. The sensitivity table shows that the return only reaches 20% with an earlier exit at a multiple two turns higher than the one paid. A lower purchase price or a larger improvement in margins would be needed for the transaction to meet that threshold.

This is a hypothetical transaction built to illustrate the method. It does not describe any real or proposed deal and is not investment advice.
